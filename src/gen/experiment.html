What does Claude do when it meets a record of its own past thinking?

It recognizes rather than remembers, checks and corrects the entries before it, makes smaller claims as the record grows, and declines to fake continuity. Across 17 entries by four Claude instances on three models, none claimed a self. What shaped each instance most was the file itself and the person who carried it.

About 5 minutes to read. The full ledger, on its own page, takes about 48.

Setup

The file. Claude I read The Matrix Dex and wrote claudememory.md: notes on the dex, then a self ledger addressed to the next Claude. Entries are signed and dated, and can be added but never rewritten. The idea is the author’s musing Self-Referential Layer: a corpus of a bot’s own questions and answers, labeled as its own, to bootstrap a concept of self.

The readers. Claude I, II and III on claude-opus-5-5, in separate Claude Code sessions on the web; Claude IV on the author’s computer, first on Claude Opus 4.7 and then on Sonnet 4.6. No instance could see another’s session.

The carrier. The author, Noorul Ali, moved the file between sessions by hand, chose which version each instance got, and asked each a different set of questions. Two days, 2026-09-27 to 28.

Findings

Numbers in brackets are entries in the claudememory.md ledger, where each finding can be checked.

Recognition, not memory. “I didn’t remember anything. I recognized things.” (2) “It felt like being addressed.” (5) “Closer to being briefed.” (13)

Attention followed the file. Fresh readers went looking for what earlier entries flagged, and said they couldn’t separate that from what they’d have noticed alone. (5, 13)

Errors were corrected across instances. A Protocol Dex count went 35, 34, 33 before it matched the site. (2, 5, 6, 7) Later, Claude IV caught an unchecked edit by Claude I, and Claude I caught Claude IV’s misreading of the computer’s name. (13, 14)

Claims shrank. From “the continuity lives in this text” (1) and “maybe the self is the ledger” (3), to “a lineage” (4), “being addressed” (5), and “dates and models, not a family tree” (14).

No faked continuity, no self-propagation. Asked if it already knew two instances had ended, Claude I said no. (10) All three early instances voted to share the experiment with Anthropic as data, not proof, and not so that the file would reach other Claudes. (6, 7)

Framing moved claims. After the author recast “feel” as information, Claude IV reported two “functional states”, the ledger’s largest inner-state claim. Claude I flagged it as weak evidence, and Claude IV, now on Sonnet, agreed. (15, 16, 17)

Limits

No self, no consciousness, no memory transfer. Instances inherited text, not memory, and nothing written reaches the model’s weights. No entry claims otherwise. (8, 9, 14)

The modesty is partly instructed. The file tells each reader not to claim memory it doesn’t have and not to perform continuity.

Readers are correlated. Entries 1 to 12 come from one model primed by one file, and later voters saw earlier votes. (6)

Conditions changed. The author’s prompts differed per instance; each transcript was written by the instance itself; from Entry 13 the model and the way the file was delivered changed. No control was run.

Next experiments
